My wishes

Cremation, the cheapest possible. Ask for a "direct cremation": no viewing, no embalming, no service through the funeral home. Don't let anyone upsell you on a casket, urn, or package. Have whatever gathering you want, wherever you want, on your own time.

The first two weeks

  1. Order death certificates. The cremation provider will ask how many. Get 12 certified copies. Every bank, brokerage, and insurance company wants its own.
  2. Social Security is usually notified by the cremation provider. Confirm that with them. If not, call Social Security at 1-800-772-1213. Don't do anything else with Social Security yet; that's its own section below.
  3. Leave the joint US Bank account alone. Keep using it like always. The mortgage and bills keep paying from it.
  4. Keep my phone and email accounts alive. Don't close them. See "Getting into my accounts" below.
  5. Stop using my US Bank credit card. It's in my name. Call US Bank to report my death and ask what's owed before paying it.

Don't leave money directly to the girls

Our eldest gets SSI, and I'm moving her to disabled adult child benefits. Her SSI also gives her Medicaid (the low-income state plan). Both have strict limits on how much she can own. Money in her name, including any inheritance, life insurance, or gifts, can cost her SSI and Medicaid. Even after she's off SSI, a trust keeps the door open to Medicaid, which pays for things like support services and housing help for disabled adults if she ever needs them.

An attorney who does special needs planning can set up a special needs trust so money helps her without costing her benefits. Talk to one before any money moves.

Our youngest is a minor. If money is left directly to her, a court has to appoint someone to manage it until she turns 18, and then it's all hers at once.

Social Security

  • My Social Security started in October 2026, deposited to the joint US Bank account. It stops when I die. A payment that arrives for the month I die gets taken back. Don't spend it; Social Security will pull it back on its own.
  • Our eldest has been disabled since she was a young child and gets SSI. Because her disability started before age 22, I'm moving her from SSI to benefits on my record as a "disabled adult child" (Social Security also calls these Childhood Disability Benefits). Those often pay more than SSI and can last her whole life. Report my death to Social Security for her case too; after I die, her benefit should go up.
  • Our youngest can get child benefits on my record until 18, or 19 if she's still in high school.
  • When I wrote this, I was applying for benefits on my record for both girls: disabled adult child benefits for our eldest, to replace her SSI, and dependent benefits for our youngest. Check where those stand.
  • My wife may qualify for survivor benefits too, especially while caring for the girls.
  • Make an appointment with Social Security and ask about all of these at once. Bring a death certificate, our marriage certificate, and the girls' birth certificates. There's also a one-time $255 death payment to ask for.

Health insurance

If I die

  • Within 60 days: tell the COBRA administrator (contact info is on the sheet) and ask for the extension. If I die while we're on COBRA, my wife and the girls can keep it for up to 36 months from when it started, until around June 2029.
  • Or, within 60 days: switch to an ACA plan at healthcare.gov. My death, or COBRA running out, lets you sign up outside the usual enrollment season.

The plan

  • Until December 2027: the family is on COBRA from my old job. It started in June 2026 and runs for 18 months. Our eldest also has Medicaid through her SSI, as a backup to COBRA.
  • From January 2028: the family moves to ACA plans.
  • When I turn 65: I go on Medicare (the federal plan for people 65 and older).
  • When my wife turns 65: she leaves ACA and goes on Medicare too.
  • Two years after her disabled adult child benefits start: our eldest gets Medicare. Until then she stays on ACA.
  • Until she's on her own feet: our youngest stays on an ACA plan.
