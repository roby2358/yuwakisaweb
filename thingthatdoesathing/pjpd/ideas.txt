Levels built from goals: the game keeps ladders of rising goals along several scales, each level deals 3 of them, and once all 3 are met the player chooses to end the level or keep going. This pairs with goal-gen-tz9e, which is the pool of goal kinds; this idea is the structure that hands those goals out.

1. Goal ladders: one list of rising targets per scale, kept like today's milestone ladder (10, 20, 50, 100, …). Candidate scales: crates delivered, one color delivered, big crates delivered, gremlins trapped, ex-gremlins, Things built or standing, construction feats (Belt run length, Bridge crossings, Spring/Cannon hops), turns without a lost crate. The player's place on each ladder carries from level to level, so every level asks for the next rung up.
2. Level start: the level opens with 3 goals drawn from different scales, each at the player's next rung on that scale, shown somewhere persistent (the tally? a goals panel?).
3. Completion: when all 3 are met, ask "End the level or continue?" Ending banks the result and starts a new level (new board? same board?). Continuing keeps the board running, maybe with fresh goals or a bonus ladder.

Open questions:
- Does this replace the endless milestone run or sit beside it as a mode?
- What does ending a level bank: a score, the turn count, unlocks that carry over (cards, board sizes), a star rating?
- Does continuing pay anything, and is there a reason to stop (risk, diminishing returns), or is it just more play?
- Is the draw seeded by Rando so it's reproducible? Can a player reroll or swap a goal?
- How do the card UNLOCKS map onto levels: per level, or by ladder rung?
- Where does progress across levels live (localStorage; per-viewer only)?
- Board size per level: does it grow or does the player pick it?

Needs an interview before it becomes tasks. Probably settle it together with goal-gen-tz9e.

A Bomb card: a one-shot card that clears the Things from several hexes at once, rather than removing them one by one with D (one command, so one tick, per Thing). Useful for tearing out a failed contraption or clearing room for a rebuild.

Design calls to settle:
- Area: the target hex plus its 6 neighbors (radius 1)? A line of hexes along the ghost's facing (R turns it, like the movers)? A radius that grows with milestones?
- What it clears: Things only, or pieces too? Crates (lost? counted in the tally?), gremlins (killed, counting as ex-gremlins?). Terrain stays; sources and targets are never touched.
- Placement: any hex, or only hexes holding a Thing? Is the card used up after one shot (yes, it's a card)? It costs one tick like any command.
- Rarity and unlock: where it sits in UNLOCKS and how often it's dealt (rare like the Pusher/Puller, D-32?). How it relates to B (blast on the hovered crate) and D (remove the selected Thing, D-39). Does Bomb make D less needed, or do they live side by side?
- Engine: a new `bomb` command, or `place` with a card whose kind is an effect rather than a Thing (data-driven, e.g. a `blastRadius` field; not `clears`, which the Cannon already uses; and not a kind check). Events: one remove/fade per cleared hex, so the animation can play it as a burst.
- UI: the ghost previews the blast area (outline every hex it will clear), so you can't misfire.

Docs when built: ONTOLOGY (command and rule), CRITERIA row, RATIONALE D-n, README card list, help legend.
